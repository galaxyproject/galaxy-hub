## Open Science across five Science Clusters

OSCARS — Open Science Clusters' Action for Research & Society — fosters the
uptake of Open Science in Europe by advancing and integrating FAIR research data
and cross-disciplinary services within
[EOSC](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/open-science-cloud).

It brings together European Research Infrastructures from the
[ESFRI](https://www.esfri.eu) roadmap and beyond, representing the five
[Science Clusters](https://science-clusters.eu): humanities and social sciences,
life sciences, environmental sciences, photon and neutron science, and
astronomy, nuclear and particle physics.

The project started on 1 January 2024 and is financed by the European Commission
through the European Research Executive Agency's HORIZON-INFRA-2023-EOSC-01-01
funding line, under
[grant agreement 101129751](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101129751).

OSCARS also runs open calls that fund small Open Science projects through
cascading grants, across repositories; data annotation; software, AI and tools;
and community building and training.

- Project website: [oscars-project.eu](https://oscars-project.eu/)

## Galaxy's part in it

The [Albert-Ludwigs-University Freiburg](https://uni-freiburg.de) and the
[Freiburg Galaxy team](/freiburg/people/)
[joined OSCARS in 2024](/news/2024-02-06-oscars-project-starting/). Where
[EuroScienceGateway](/projects/esg/) built the federated compute, OSCARS is an
opportunity to connect Galaxy to communities well beyond the life sciences —
Galaxy is, in principle, domain-agnostic, and OSCARS is a good test of that.

Work connected to OSCARS on the Hub includes the
[Galaxy Imaging Hackathon](/news/12-05-2025-galaxy-imaging-hackathon2025/),
[Voronoi segmentation for astronomy](/news/2025-06-11-voronoi-astronomy/), and
the cross-disciplinary reuse of BioImage.IO AI models in Galaxy, which was
[selected for the RAISE Registry of Good Practices in AI-enabled Science](/news/2026-10-21-raise-registry/).
