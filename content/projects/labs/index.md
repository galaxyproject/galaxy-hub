## A Galaxy tailored to a research community

A Galaxy Lab is a customised "skin" over an existing Galaxy server: an
alternative landing page and toolbox, served from a subdomain of the base site —
for example [microbiology.usegalaxy.eu](https://microbiology.usegalaxy.eu/). The
Galaxy underneath is unchanged, with the same accounts, histories and compute;
what differs is what a researcher lands on and how much of the toolbox they have
to wade through to get started.

To build one, or to read the Labs Engine documentation, start at
**[labs.usegalaxy.org.au](https://labs.usegalaxy.org.au/)**.

The curated toolbox is a Galaxy
[tool panel view](https://docs.galaxyproject.org/en/master/admin/tool_panel.html#tool-panel-views):
a subset of the server's tools, arranged in a structure the community chooses
rather than the server-wide default.

The landing page itself can be any HTML page, but is usually generated by the
**Galaxy Labs Engine**, which keeps Labs consistent with one another and
portable between servers. The Engine renders a Lab for the server that requests
it, so tool links point at the Galaxy the page is being viewed on and
server-specific content is adjusted. A Lab can carry a per-server file that
overrides parts of its content for one site while inheriting the rest, which is
how a single Lab serves Galaxy Main, Galaxy Europe and Galaxy Australia at once.

## Community-built content

The content of each Lab is built by the community itself, not by server
administrators. A Lab is a set of Markdown or HTML snippets structured with
YAML: a root file naming the sections of tools, workflows and training, and the
buttons and text around them. Lab content for a number of communities lives in
[Galaxy CoDex](https://github.com/galaxyproject/galaxy_codex), and anyone can
propose a change to it with a pull request.

The Engine fetches that content and renders it on request, so a merged change
reaches every server hosting the Lab within minutes — no redeployment, no
release cycle, and no need to ask an administrator. Removing the technical
gatekeepers from Lab maintenance was the point: the people who know a research
domain best are the ones editing its Lab.

## Labs in the wild

Labs grew out of the Galaxy Europe "flavours" that gave a domain its own
subdomain, and now run across the three largest public servers — among them
[microbiology.usegalaxy.eu](https://microbiology.usegalaxy.eu/), the
[Single Cell](/community/sig/singlecell/) Lab at
[singlecell.usegalaxy.eu](https://singlecell.usegalaxy.eu/) and on Galaxy Main,
and the Australian genome Lab at
[genome.usegalaxy.org.au](https://genome.usegalaxy.org.au/) built with the
Threatened Species Initiative.

## Building a Lab

The Labs Engine is led by the [Galaxy Australia](/use/usegalaxy-au/) team with
the Australian BioCommons. Its generator and documentation are at
[labs.usegalaxy.org.au](https://labs.usegalaxy.org.au/), which will walk you
through a new Lab from a template.

For the story of how Labs came about, see
[Reflections on the journey behind the Galaxy Labs Engine paper](/news/2026-05-14-galaxy-labs-engine-reflections/).
