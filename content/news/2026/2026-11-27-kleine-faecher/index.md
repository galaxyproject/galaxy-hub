# AI4SOCIAL+ joins the conversation on AI and the future of small subjects
How can smaller academic fields make the most of AI, without losing what makes their research distinctive? On Friday 20 November, the EU project [AI4SOCIAL+](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101292886) joined that conversation in Mainz.
Daniela Schneider of Galaxy Europe at the University of Freiburg presented AI4SOCIAL+ with a poster at the World Café of the conference "Künstliche Intelligenz und die Zukunft kleiner Fächer" (AI and the future of small subjects).

# About the conference
The two-day conference, held on 19–20 November 2026, looked at how smaller fields can handle the impact of AI on their methods and research. 
It was organised by the Arbeitsstelle Kleine Fächer and the Akademie der Wissenschaften und der Literatur in Mainz, with funding from NFDI4Culture, Kultusministerkonferenz and Stabstelle Gleichstellung und Diversität JGU.
"Small subjects" (kleine Fächer) are disciplines with only a handful of professorships, such as papyrology, sign language research, numismatics or ancient Near Eastern studies. 
They often work with rare, complex or sparse data, which makes the arrival of AI both a big opportunity and a real challenge.
The first day brought perspectives from inside the disciplines and from research infrastructure and policy, with a keynote by Anne Krüger of the Weizenbaum Institute on how generative AI is changing scientific practice. The second day opened with a World Café on AI in the everyday research of small subjects: a marketplace of methods, infrastructures and networks, with contributors from disciplines, academies and NFDI consortia.
You can find out more on the [conference website](https://www.kleinefaecher.de/beitraege/blogbeitrag/tagung-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-die-zukunft-kleiner-faecher-am-19-und-20-november-2026).

# What we bring to the World Café
At the World Café, Daniela showed how Galaxy can support researchers in small subjects. 
Galaxy is an open-source platform for data analysis that gives researchers access to tools and workflows, including AI tools, through a web browser and without programming.
She also introduced AI4SOCIAL+. In the project, the Galaxy team supports our five use cases on AI readiness, helping turn research questions from the social sciences and humanities into reproducible, AI-ready workflows.

# Why it matters for AI readiness
AI4SOCIAL+ is building an AI readiness framework, tools and infrastructure for European research, starting with the social sciences and humanities. 
Small subjects indicate AI's real applicability to research. If AI readiness works for fields with specialised data and small communities, it can work across research.
Conferences like these are as much about listening as presenting. It was great to hear what researchers in small subjects need from AI and from research infrastructure, and where AI4SOCIAL+ can help.
