# PAG 34

The 34th International Plant and Animal Genome Conference (PAG 34) takes place **January 8–12, 2027**, at the Town & Country Resort and Convention Center in **San Diego, California**. The conference brings together thousands of researchers, exhibitors, and genomicists for plenary sessions, workshops, poster presentations, and an exhibit hall.

The Galaxy Project will be at PAG 34 with both a **workshop** and an **exhibition booth**.

## Galaxy Workshop at PAG 34

- **When:** Sunday, January 10, 2027, 4:00–6:10 PM
- **Where:** Town and Country D
- **Session page:** [PAG 34 program listing](https://plan.core-apps.com/pag34/event/e9debaf0f0da81243899608b28c0c87b)

[Galaxy](https://usegalaxy.org) is an open-source, web-based platform for accessible, reproducible data analysis. This workshop will introduce Galaxy and highlight recent updates for running, sharing, and reusing genomic workflows. We will examine Galaxy's scientific impact through published research, highlighting the questions researchers have addressed with the platform and how its use has expanded across disciplines.

We will demonstrate AI-assisted analysis with Galaxy and [Orbit](https://galaxyproject.github.io/orbit/). Starting from a research question, Orbit can propose an analysis plan, find existing Galaxy tools and workflows, and run the analysis after researcher review. Galaxy histories capture the inputs, tools, and parameters used to generate the results.

The workshop will conclude with genome assembly and pangenome analysis. We will highlight how the [Vertebrate Genomes Project workflows in Galaxy](/projects/vgp/workflows/) use long reads and Hi-C data to build and evaluate phased, chromosome-scale assemblies. We will then present recent work with [Panagram](https://github.com/kjenike/panagram), which uses shared k-mers to compare collections of assemblies without whole-genome alignment. Examples from 160 *Arabidopsis* genomes and a comparative analysis across 605 vertebrate genomes will illustrate how Panagram identifies conserved and variable sequences and tests for associations with environmental variables and phenotypic traits.
