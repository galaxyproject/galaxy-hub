Make more Bioconductor analysis methods accessible through Galaxy and other workflow platforms, without having to develop a separate wrapper for each ecosystem.

The **BioFAIR Workflow Interoperability Sprint** brings together researchers, software developers, and contributors from the Bioconductor, Galaxy, Nextflow/nf-core, and other bioinformatics communities to explore [BiocJobs](https://blog.bioconductor.org/posts/2026-08-21-biocjobs/).

BiocJobs automatically generates **Galaxy tools, Nextflow modules, WDL tasks, and more** from R scripts and metadata stored directly in Bioconductor packages. Building on an initial [BioFAIR Collaboration Fest in September 2026](https://blog.bioconductor.org/posts/2026-10-07-biofair-cofest/), this event aims to put the approach to the test across more analysis methods and workflow ecosystems.

## What will we work on?

Participants will collaborate on practical tasks, including:

- **Bioconductor contributors:** Identify useful analysis methods and write R scripts exposing their functionality through BiocJobs.
- **Galaxy contributors:** Test and improve generated Galaxy tool wrappers, assess compatibility with Galaxy conventions, and explore integration into existing workflows.
- **Nextflow and other workflow developers:** Evaluate generated modules and tasks, identify limitations, and contribute improvements.
- **Researchers and workflow users:** Suggest analysis methods and use cases, test generated tools, and provide feedback.

Previous work has focused on [single-cell analysis](https://github.com/kevinrue/biofair-nextflow-pipeline/tree/main/modules), with additional [examples using DESeq2](https://github.com/almahmoud/BiocJobs/tree/main/examples/DESeq2). Contributions from other research domains are particularly welcome.

## Prerequisites

Participants are welcome regardless of their familiarity with BiocJobs. Experience with at least one of the following would be useful:

- R and Bioconductor packages
- Galaxy tools and workflows
- Nextflow/nf-core or other workflow systems
- Computational analysis methods and reproducible research

**You don't need to be an expert in all these technologies to contribute.** The event is designed to bring together complementary expertise across communities.
