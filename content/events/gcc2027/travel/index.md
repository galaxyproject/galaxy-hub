# Travel

GCC2027 is being held at the **[Université de Sherbrooke – Campus de Longueuil](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/campus-longueuil/)**, located at **150 Place Charles-Le Moyne, Longueuil, QC J4K 0A8, Canada**.

Situated on the South Shore (*Rive-Sud*) of the Saint Lawrence River, the campus is directly connected indoors to the **Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke Metro station** (Yellow Line / *Ligne jaune*), placing attendees just a 10-minute subway ride from the heart of downtown **Montréal**.

## About Longueuil & the Greater Montréal Area

### Longueuil & Montréal: Riverside Convenience Meets Cosmopolitan Culture

Hosting GCC2027 in **Longueuil** gives attendees the best of both worlds: a modern, welcoming riverside hub on the South Shore with immediate, effortless access to **Montréal**—North America's premier French-speaking metropolis.

Directly across the Saint Lawrence River from Montréal, **Longueuil** (population ~255,000; over 430,000 in its agglomeration) combines urban convenience with a relaxed neighbourhood feel. Just minutes from the conference venue, the historic district of **Vieux-Longueuil** along **Rue Saint-Charles** charms visitors with heritage stone buildings, leafy *terrasses*, independent bistros, and lively microbreweries, while **Parc Marie-Victorin** offers scenic walking and cycling paths along the riverfront.

A single short ride under the river on the Metro's Yellow Line brings you straight into **Montréal**, a vibrant island city of over 4.3 million people in its metropolitan area. Famous for its blend of **European charm and North American energy**, Montréal enchants visitors with the cobblestone streets of **Old Montréal (*Vieux-Montréal*)**, the panoramic trails of **Mount Royal**, creative neighbourhoods like the **Plateau Mont-Royal** and **Mile End**, and a world-renowned summer festival scene.

For inspiration on what to see and do around the conference, check out our **[Highlights in Montréal & Longueuil](#highlights-in-montréal--longueuil)** section below!

### Weather in Late June & Early July

Late June and early July mark the height of summer in southern Québec, bringing **warm, sunny days and lively outdoor energy**:

- **Temperatures**: Daytime highs average **24°C to 27°C (75°F to 81°F)**, with comfortable evenings around **15°C to 18°C (59°F to 64°F)**. Occasional warmer, humid days can reach 30°C (86°F) or higher.
- **What to Pack**: Bring **light, breathable summer clothing**, **comfortable walking shoes**, and **sun protection** (sunglasses, sunscreen, and a hat). Because summer weather can occasionally bring a brief afternoon shower or thunderstorm—and indoor conference lecture halls are air-conditioned—packing a **light sweater or jacket** and a **rain coat** is recommended.
- **Long Daylight Hours**: With the sun setting around **8:45 PM**, you will have plenty of daylight after conference sessions to stroll along downtown Montréal, or unwind on an outdoor *terrasse*.

## Getting to Longueuil & Montréal

### By Air

Most international and domestic attendees will arrive via **[Montréal–Pierre Elliott Trudeau International Airport (YUL)](https://www.admtl.com/en)**, located in Dorval about **20 km west of downtown Montréal** (approximately 40 minutes by car (without traffic) or 55 minutes by public transit to Campus de Longueuil). YUL offers many direct flights to over 150 destinations across North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, and Latin America.

#### From YUL Airport to Campus de Longueuil by Public Transit

Reaching the conference venue from YUL by public transit is straightforward and affordable:

1. **Take the [747 Airport Express Bus](https://www.stm.info/en/info/networks/bus/shuttle/747-yul-montreal-trudeau-airport-downtown-shuttle)**: Operated by the STM, the 747 bus runs **24 hours a day, 7 days a week** from the airport arrivals level right into downtown Montréal. Stay on the bus until its terminus at **Station Berri-UQAM** (~40–50 minutes depending on traffic).
2. **Transfer to the Metro (Yellow Line)**: Inside **Berri-UQAM**, follow the signs for the **Yellow Line (*Ligne jaune* – Direction Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke)**. The train crosses under the Saint Lawrence River and arrives at **Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke** in just **2 stops (~8–10 minutes)**, directly beneath the conference venue.
3. **Tickets**: A 747 bus ticket costs **$11 CAD** and includes 24 hours of unlimited travel across the STM network (Zone A), which covers boarding the Yellow Line at Berri-UQAM to reach Longueuil. Purchase your ticket from the fare vending machines in the airport international arrivals area **before** boarding the bus. *(See [Public Transportation & Getting Around](#public-transportation--getting-around) below for important details on Zone AB return fares from Longueuil.)*

#### From YUL Airport by Taxi or Rideshare

- **Taxis**: Licensed taxis are available at the central rank outside the arrivals level at YUL—no reservation required. Taxis operate on a fixed rate to downtown Montréal and a metered rate across the river to Longueuil (typically **$65 to $85 CAD** depending on traffic). Credit and debit cards are accepted.
- **Rideshare**: **Uber**, **Lyft**, and local electric taxi service **Téo Taxi** operate from designated pickup zones at the airport arrivals level.

#### Regional Option: Montréal Metropolitan Airport (YHU)

For domestic travelers flying within Canada, **[Montréal Metropolitan Airport (YHU)](https://metropolitanairport.ca/)** in Saint-Hubert (Longueuil) is located just **15 minutes by taxi or bus** from Campus de Longueuil and serves regional Canadian routes.

### By Train

Traveling by train is a comfortable, scenic, and low-carbon option for attendees arriving from eastern Canada or the northeastern United States:

- **[VIA Rail Canada](https://www.viarail.ca/en)**: Frequent daily trains connect **Québec City, Ottawa, Kingston, and Toronto** to downtown Montréal's **[Gare Centrale (Central Station)](https://www.viarail.ca/en/explore-our-destinations/stations/quebec/montreal)**. Trains on the Québec City–Montréal corridor also stop at **[Saint-Lambert Station](https://www.viarail.ca/en/explore-our-destinations/stations/quebec/st-lambert)** on the South Shore, which is just a 10-minute taxi or local bus ride from Campus de Longueuil.
- **[Amtrak (*Adirondack*)](https://www.amtrak.com/adirondack-train)**: Offers a daily scenic rail journey from **New York City (Penn Station)** through the Hudson Valley and along Lake Champlain, stopping at both **Saint-Lambert** and **Montréal Gare Centrale**.

**From Montréal Gare Centrale to the Venue (~20 minutes)**: Follow the indoor signs to the **Bonaventure Metro station** (Orange Line), take the train 4 stops toward *Montmorency* to **Berri-UQAM**, and transfer to the **Yellow Line** to **Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke**.

### By Car

Longueuil sits at the crossroads of major Québec, Canadian, and U.S. highways, right at the southern approach of the **Jacques-Cartier Bridge**:

- **From Ontario & Western Québec (Ottawa / Toronto)**: Follow **Autoroute 20 East** (or Autoroute 40 East to Autoroute 25 South via the Louis-Hippolyte-La Fontaine Tunnel) onto **Route 132 West/East** in Longueuil to Place Charles-Le Moyne.
- **From Québec City & Eastern Québec**: Take **Autoroute 20 West** directly to Exit 89 / **Route 132** toward Longueuil and the Jacques-Cartier Bridge.
- **From Sherbrooke & New England (Vermont / Boston via I-89 or I-91)**: Take **Autoroute 10 West** (or Autoroute 35 North to Autoroute 10 West) across the South Shore, following signs for **Route 134 North (Boulevard Taschereau)** toward the Jacques-Cartier Bridge / Place Charles-Le Moyne.
- **From New York State (via I-87 North)**: I-87 becomes **Autoroute 15 North** at the Canadian border; merge onto **Route 132 East** on the South Shore directly to Longueuil.

**Parking**: Paid underground and surface parking lots are available directly at **Place Charles-Le Moyne / Campus de Longueuil** (accessible via Rue Saint-Charles Ouest and Place Charles-Le Moyne).

## Public Transportation & Getting Around

One of the greatest advantages of the GCC2027 venue is its direct indoor integration with Greater Montréal's transit network.

### Metro (STM Yellow Line)

The conference venue sits directly above the **[Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke Metro station](https://www.stm.info/en/info/networks/metro/longueuil-universite-de-sherbrooke)**, the southern terminus of the **Yellow Line (*Ligne jaune*)**:

- **1 stop (2 minutes)** to **Parc Jean-Drapeau** (Île Sainte-Hélène / Biosphere).
- **2 stops (8–10 minutes)** to **Berri-UQAM**, the central transfer station in downtown Montréal connecting to the **Green Line (*Ligne verte*)** and **Orange Line (*Ligne orange*)**.

Metros run every few minutes from approximately **5:30 AM to 1:00 AM** (until 1:30 AM on Saturdays).

### Transit Fares & Zones (Important Local Tip!)

Public transit across Greater Montréal is coordinated by the **[Autorité régionale de transport métropolitain (ARTM)](https://www.artm.quebec/en/)** using a fare zone system:

- **Island of Montréal** is **Zone A**.
- **Longueuil (South Shore)** is **Zone B**.

<div class="callout">
<strong>Which Metro ticket to buy:</strong> To travel by Metro between Longueuil and Montréal, you need an <strong>All Modes AB (<em>Tous modes AB</em>)</strong> fare. Standard "Zone A" Montréal tourist passes cover buses and Metro stations on the Island of Montréal (and allow you to exit at Longueuil when boarding in Montréal), but <strong>will not open the turnstiles to board the Metro at Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke station</strong>. Always select <strong>All Modes AB</strong> (available as 1-trip, 2-trip, 10-trip, 24-hour, 3-day, or weekly passes) at station vending machines!

You can plan routes in real time using the **[Chrono mobile app](https://www.artm.quebec/en/chrono-mobile-application/)**, Google Maps, or the locally developed **[Transit app](https://transitapp.com/)**.

### South Shore Buses (RTL)

The **[Réseau de transport de Longueuil (RTL)](https://www.rtl-longueuil.qc.ca/en-CA/)** bus terminal is located inside the same complex as the Metro station and Campus de Longueuil, offering frequent bus service along Rue Saint-Charles (Vieux-Longueuil), across the South Shore, and to nearby national parks.

### Cycling (BIXI) & River Shuttle

- **[BIXI Bike Share](https://bixi.com/en/)**: Greater Montréal's popular bike-sharing network features hundreds of stations—including standard and electric-assist bikes—right outside Campus de Longueuil and throughout Montréal. Experienced cyclists can even ride between Longueuil and Montréal via the scenic multipurpose path across the **Jacques-Cartier Bridge** or along the **Saint Lawrence Seaway path** through Parc Jean-Drapeau.
- **[River Shuttle (*Navette fluviale*)](https://navark.ca/en/river-shuttles/)**: Throughout the summer, pedestrian and cyclist ferries connect the **Longueuil Marina (Parc Marie-Victorin)**, **Parc Jean-Drapeau**, and the **Old Port of Montréal (*Vieux-Port*)**, offering a relaxing and scenic commute on the water.

## Culinary Scene

### Québécois & Montréal Specialties

Montréal and Québec boast one of North America's most celebrated culinary scenes, blending French culinary heritage, hearty Québécois comfort food, Indigenous traditions, and flavours from around the globe.

Must-try local classics include:

- ***Poutine***: Québec's iconic dish of crispy golden fries topped with fresh, "squeaky" cheese curds (*fromage en grains*) and rich, warm gravy—served everywhere from classic *casse-croûtes* to gourmet bistros (with legendary spots like *La Banquise*, *Ma Poule Mouillée*, or *Chez Tousignant*).
- ***Montréal-Style Bagels***: Smaller, denser, and sweeter than New York bagels, hand-rolled, poached in honey-sweetened water, coated in sesame or poppy seeds, and baked in wood-fired ovens. Picking up a warm bag fresh from the oven in Mile End (*St-Viateur Bagel* or *Fairmount Bagel*, both open 24 hours) is a quintessential local ritual.
- ***Montréal Smoked Meat (Viande fumée)***: Beef brisket cured for days with a savoury blend of cracked peppercorns and spices, steamed to tender perfection, hand-sliced, and piled high on rye bread with yellow mustard.
- ***Artisanal Québec Cheeses & Charcuterie***: Québec produces hundreds of award-winning fine cheeses—from creamy *Riopelle de l'Isle* and *Oka* to aged *Louis d'Or* and *Bleu Bénédictin*.
- ***Maple Treats (Sirop d'érable) & Pastries***: Québec produces over 70% of the world's maple syrup. Be sure to try traditional desserts like ***pouding chômeur*** (warm cake baked in maple syrup sauce) or ***tarte au sucre*** (maple sugar pie), alongside French-style *viennoiseries* from local bakeries.

### Markets, Microbreweries & Dining Out

For an authentic taste of local life, visit one of the area's bustling public markets—especially in **late June, which is peak local strawberry (*fraises du Québec*) season**:

- **[Jean-Talon Market (*Marché Jean-Talon*)](https://www.marchespublics-mtl.com/en/marches/jean-talon-market/)** (Little Italy, Orange/Blue Metro line): One of the largest open-air farmers' markets in North America, surrounded by espresso bars, cheese shops, and specialty food stalls.
- **[Atwater Market (*Marché Atwater*)](https://www.marchespublics-mtl.com/en/marches/atwater-market/)** (Saint-Henri, Green Metro line): Famous for its Art Deco market hall right next to the scenic **Lachine Canal** bike and walking path.
- **[Marché public de Longueuil](https://marchepublicdelongueuil.com/)**: Located on the South Shore, showcasing regional Montérégie farm produce, ciders, and baked goods.

Québec is also a pioneer of the **craft beer (*microbrasserie*)** movement and is internationally known for **Québec ice cider (*cidre de glace*)** and artisanal ciders from the neighbouring Montérégie orchards. Whether you are grabbing a casual pint on **Rue Saint-Charles in Longueuil** or exploring the neighbourhoods of Montréal, you will find exceptional local brews and welcoming *terrasses*.

## Practical Information for Travelers

- **Language**: **French** is the official language of Québec, and Greater Montréal is deeply bilingual. You will be warmly welcomed in both French and English at hotels, restaurants, transit stations, and shops (often greeted with the classic Montréal *"Bonjour-Hi!"*), though a friendly *"Bonjour"* and *"Merci"* are always appreciated.
- **Currency & Payments**: The local currency is the **Canadian Dollar ($ CAD)**. Contactless credit and debit cards (**Visa**, **Mastercard**, **Apple Pay**, **Google Pay**) are accepted almost everywhere, from taxis and metro vending machines to bakeries and coffee shops.
- **Taxes & Tipping**: Displayed prices in shops and on restaurant menus do **not** include sales taxes—federal **GST (5%)** and provincial **QST (9.975%)**, totaling **~14.975%**, are added at checkout. In sit-down restaurants, bars, and taxis, tipping is customary (typically **15% to 18%** of the pre-tax bill).
- **Electricity**: Canada uses standard North American **Type A and Type B** electrical outlets (**120V, 60Hz**). Travelers arriving from outside North America should bring a plug adapter.
